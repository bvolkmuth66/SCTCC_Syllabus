Getting started

What is the syllabus builder?

A web page for making your SCTCC course syllabus. You fill in your course information in the Edit syllabus panel, and the builder adds the current college policies and statements automatically. When you are done, you print it to a PDF and upload the PDF to D2L.

The college wording is kept up to date centrally, so you never need to copy policy text from an old syllabus.

What do I need to use it?

Only a web browser. Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge work best: they can save straight to a folder you choose and they make accessible PDFs. There is nothing to install and no account to sign in to.

How should I start the first time?

When the builder opens, it asks How would you like to start?

  • Import a previous syllabus (Word or PDF) if you have a syllabus from an earlier term. Most of your course information is filled in for you.
  • Open a syllabus saved from this builder after your first term.
  • Start from scratch for a new course.
  • See an example to look at a finished sample course.

You can get back to these choices at any time with Start over… in Save and open your syllabus.

Where should the course description and outcomes come from?

Copy them from the official SCTCC Course Outline for your course. The same applies to credits, prerequisites, corequisites, and the Minnesota Transfer Curriculum goal area. Outcomes appear under a Student Learning Outcomes heading as a bulleted list.

The editor gets very long. How do I close a section?

Click Collapse this section at the bottom of the section, or click its heading at the top. Collapsing a section only hides it in the editor; nothing you typed is lost, and it still appears in the syllabus.

What do the section numbers mean?

Each group in the Edit syllabus panel shows the number of the syllabus section it fills in, for example 2.2 Materials and technology. The same numbers appear in the preview and in the PDF, so you can find what you are looking at. The numbers update when you turn optional sections on or off.

What does “[… needed]” in the preview mean?

Something required is still empty, for example [Withdrawal date needed]. These markers show only in your preview, never in the PDF. Save as PDF will not continue until they are filled in. Check my syllabus at the top of the editor lists them all, each with a Go to field button.

Saving and your files

Where is my syllabus saved?

Only where you save it. Your syllabus is your intellectual property, so the builder never keeps a copy: it does not use browser storage, cookies, GitHub, or any server, and nothing is uploaded. When you close the page, the only copies are the files you saved.

Click Save syllabus (or press Ctrl+S, or ⌘S on a Mac) and choose a folder on your computer or your OneDrive folder. After the first save, Save syllabus updates the same file without asking again.

How do I save to OneDrive?

When you click Save syllabus, choose your OneDrive folder in the Save window. It appears in File Explorer (Windows) or Finder (Mac) when the OneDrive app is installed. OneDrive then keeps the file in the cloud for you.

What kind of file is saved?

A .json file named after your course, for example CPTR-1118-Spring-2027-Syllabus.json. It holds everything you entered, including your photo, so you can open it again in the builder next term. It is not the syllabus students see: that is the PDF.

Why did my file go to the Downloads folder?

Firefox and Safari cannot save directly to a folder you choose, so there Save syllabus downloads the file instead. Move it from Downloads to your own folder or OneDrive. Chrome and Edge let you choose the folder.

What happens if I close the page without saving?

Your changes are lost, because the builder does not keep a copy. The status line at the top of the editor shows whether your latest changes are saved, and the browser warns you if you try to close the page with unsaved changes.

How do I start next term’s syllabus from this one?

  1. Click Open a saved syllabus and choose last term’s file.
  2. Click Save as… and give the new term’s file a new name, so last term’s file stays as it was.
  3. Under Credits, modality, and dates, choose the new term and click Fill in these dates.
  4. In Course calendar, use Shift dates for a new term… to move your calendar forward (see How do I move last year’s calendar to this year?).

What is the difference between “Save syllabus” and “Save as PDF”?

Save syllabus saves your working file (.json), which you open again in the builder to make changes or to start next term’s syllabus. Save as PDF makes the finished, accessible PDF that students see in D2L. Keep both.

The year I need isn’t in the Year list.

The list shows this year and the next two, and moves forward each January 1. A year already in an opened syllabus stays in the list. To make next year’s syllabus from an older one, choose the new year.

What is “Last revised,” and how do I set it?

The date you last changed the syllabus. It is optional, and when filled in it prints in the PDF footer after the version, for example “Version 1.1 · Revised 11/26/2026.” Click Use today’s date beside it to fill in today.

What does “Clear this syllabus” do?

It empties the fields on screen. Your saved file is not changed unless you save afterward.

Importing an old syllabus

Can I import my old Word or PDF syllabus?

Yes. Choose Import a previous syllabus (Word or PDF). The file is read on your computer and never uploaded. A review screen lists everything found; untick anything you do not want, then click Import selected.

Word (.docx) files give the best results. For an older .doc file, open it in Word and use Save As to make a .docx first.

Why didn’t the college policy text come in with my import?

On purpose. Sections such as Academic Integrity, Accommodations, Title IX, Navigating D2L, Technical Support, Student Services, and Accessibility & Data Privacy always come from the current college content, so old wording can’t carry forward. Your own sections, such as Exams or Lab Safety, are imported as Additional course sections.

Why weren’t the dates imported?

Old dates would be wrong for the new term. Choose the new term and click Fill in these dates. If your old syllabus had a calendar table, its topics, readings, and due items are kept and placed in order when you generate the new calendar.

My PDF says it has no text to read. What can I do?

The PDF is probably a scan (a picture of the pages). Use the Word version of the syllabus if you have it. A PDF made with the Adobe PDF or Microsoft Print to PDF printers can also have no readable text. Pictures are not taken from a PDF, so upload your photo in the editor.

Editing your syllabus

Can I change the college policy wording?

No. College sections are kept centrally so every syllabus has the current, approved wording. If something in a college section is wrong or out of date, contact Magen Henry.

How do I turn an optional section on or off?

Use the checkbox for that section in the editor, for example Include my photo, or the optional college statements under Course policies. A section that is turned off does not appear in the preview or the PDF, and what you typed is kept in case you turn it back on. Required sections cannot be turned off.

How do I add my own section, such as Exams or Lab Safety?

Under Course policies, go to Additional course sections and add a section with your own heading and text.

How do I make a bulleted or numbered list?

In any text box, start each line with a bullet (•), a hyphen (-), or an asterisk (*) for a bulleted list, or with 1., 2. for a numbered list. These print as real lists, which screen readers announce as lists.

How do I add a link?

Type or paste the full web address (starting with https://) into any text box. It becomes a working link in the syllabus. Very long addresses, such as booking links, are shown shortened, for example outlook.office.com/bookwithme/…, but still go to the full address.

What is the difference between the two diversity statements?

The College Diversity Statement is required and always included. The Program Diversity Statement is optional: turn it on under Course policies if your program has its own statement. It appears right after the college statement, and each heading says whose statement it is.

What do I need to enter for an outside tool or vendor?

Under Tools and vendors, list each outside tool students use, such as a publisher’s lab site. Its accessibility statement is required. If the vendor has none, check This tool has no accessibility statement and link its VPAT (accessibility report) instead. Add the privacy policy and support link if you have them.

How do I add my photo?

Upload a PNG, JPEG, or WebP picture up to 300 KB in the instructor section. Include my photo turns on automatically. The photo is stored inside your saved syllabus file.

Grading

Which grading method should I choose?

  • Points: the final grade is total points earned out of the course total.
  • Points with set weights: each category has points and a weight you set, for example Labs are worth 40% no matter how many points they have.
  • Percentage weights only: each category has a weight and no points.

Switching methods loses nothing: fields that don’t apply are hidden but kept.

Why won’t it print when my weights don’t total 100%?

So a mistake doesn’t reach students. The editor shows the total as you type and never changes your percentages. If the total is intentional, for example for extra credit, tick Publish with weights that do not total 100% and explain it under “Additional grading information.”

Can I use my own grading scale or plus/minus grades?

Yes. The default is Use the college grading scale (A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69, F 0–59), which updates automatically if the college changes it. Choose Enter my own grading scale to change it. Use plus/minus sample fills in plus and minus grades as a starting point to confirm for your program.

Why does my grading scale show only percentages?

With Points with set weights or Percentage weights only, points don’t convert directly to the final grade, so the scale shows percentages. With Points, the matching point range for each grade is calculated for you.

Course calendar

How do I build my course calendar?

  1. Under Credits, modality, and dates, choose the term, click Fill in these dates, and tick your meeting days.
  2. In Course calendar, click Add the college’s no-class dates to add holidays and breaks.
  3. Click Generate calendar. One row is made for every class meeting.
  4. Fill in each class’s topic, reading, and due item.

In the calendar workspace, a No calendar yet box at the top has the Generate calendar button until the calendar is made. Until then the month view shows empty days: that is expected.

If your schedule is posted somewhere else, such as D2L, you can instead write that in Statement about the course schedule.

Where do the calendar dates and meeting days come from?

From Credits, modality, and dates. The calendar always runs from the course begin date to the end date, on the course meeting days, so there are no separate calendar dates to keep in step. If you change the course dates or days, generate the calendar again. Your topics are kept.

How do I add topics, readings, and due dates?

In the editor, open a calendar row to fill in Topic, Preparation/Reading, Assignment due, and Notes. In the calendar workspace, choose a class in the month view and fill them in on the right.

“Add the college’s no-class dates” doesn’t work for my term.

The college calendar in the builder only has the terms that have been published. If your term isn’t there yet, the builder says so. Add holidays and breaks yourself under No-class dates (a date and a reason, such as “Thanksgiving break”), then choose Generate calendar, or Generate calendar again if you already made it. Your topics stay with their dates.

Can I work on the calendar on a bigger screen?

Yes. In Course calendar, click Open the calendar workspace. It opens in its own window, which you can drag to a second screen, with a month view and a list view. If the browser blocks new windows, or you click Open it full screen here, it opens full screen inside the builder. Changes in the workspace and the editor are the same calendar.

How do I move last year’s calendar to this year?

Open last year’s saved syllabus, then use Shift dates for a new term… (in the editor) or Shift dates… (in the calendar workspace). It moves every date by the same amount, the way D2L does when you copy a course. Give it two dates, such as last year’s and this year’s first day of class, or a number of days. A preview shows every old and new date before anything changes.

To lay last term’s topics onto a calendar you already generated, use Reuse last term’s schedule… in the workspace instead.

My class time or room is different this term. Where do I change it?

In the Shift dates window, under Class time and room for the new term, which also shows last term’s values for comparison. You can also change them under Credits, modality, and dates. Either way the change shows everywhere in the syllabus.

Class was cancelled (a snow day or closure). How do I push the topics back?

On the cancelled class, click Shift topics later from here. That class is emptied and every later topic moves to the next class. If the last class had a topic, it becomes an unscheduled topic at the end so nothing is lost.

My course is online or meets five days a week. What layout should I use?

Choose One row per week (online courses) as the calendar layout. The meeting-by-meeting layout is for courses that meet one to four days a week.

Can I open my calendar in Excel?

Yes. Export calendar (.csv) saves it as a file that opens in Excel or Google Sheets.

Printing and the PDF

How do I make the PDF for D2L?

  1. Click Save as PDF in the green toolbar, then Continue to Save as PDF. Your browser opens its own window, titled Print.
  2. Set Destination to Save as PDF.
  3. Under More settings, turn Headers and footers off. The syllabus prints its own footer and page numbers.
  4. Save the PDF to your own folder or OneDrive, then upload it to D2L.

Why must I choose “Save as PDF” and not Adobe PDF or Microsoft Print to PDF?

The Adobe PDF and Microsoft Print to PDF printers turn the text into shapes. The PDF has no headings or reading order, its text can’t be selected or searched, and screen readers read nonsense, so the syllabus fails the accessibility requirements. Save as PDF in Chrome or Edge makes a tagged PDF that screen readers can read, with working links.

How can I tell whether my PDF is accessible?

A quick test: open the PDF and try to select a sentence of text. If you can’t select it, make the PDF again with Save as PDF. For a full check, Adobe Acrobat Pro has an accessibility checker.

Why did Save as PDF stop and show a list of problems?

Something must be fixed first, such as a missing date, template placeholder text left in your syllabus, or grading weights that don’t total 100%. Each problem has a Go to field button. Items under Please review and For your information don’t stop you from saving the PDF.

My PDF has the date and a web address printed at the top. How do I remove them?

Those come from the browser. In the browser’s window (titled Print), open More settings and turn Headers and footers off, then save the PDF again.

Troubleshooting and help

The college sections are missing or show an error. What do I do?

Make sure you opened the builder from its web address, not from a file on your computer, and that you are online. Then reload the page. If the problem continues, the message in the editor says which section failed to load; report it to the builder contact.

The FAQs or calendar window didn’t open.

Your browser may be blocking new windows. The calendar then opens full screen inside the builder instead. For the FAQs, allow pop-ups for the builder’s site, or the FAQs open in a new tab.
